## Core learning

Evidence roles must remain explicit:

DISCOVERY EVIDENCE  
→ may generate questions and hypotheses

DERIVATION EVIDENCE  
→ may shape the method, ruler, parameters, thresholds, sample rules, or outcome horizon

UNTOUCHED EVALUATION EVIDENCE  
→ may challenge a frozen method but must not have been used to create, tune, select, or rescue it

REPLICATION EVIDENCE  
→ later challenges a finding under a meaningfully distinct evidence condition

## Earned principles

- Discovery is not confirmation.
- Evidence used to create, tune, select, or rescue a method cannot honestly be called independent confirmation of that same method.
- Out-of-sample does not automatically mean untouched.
- Random splitting is not automatically valid for dependent time-series evidence.
- Temporal, cross-sectional, and source-based partitions answer different questions.
- Different providers can test source robustness without creating a second market history.
- Once an evaluation set is consulted to revise or choose a method, it becomes derivation evidence for that revised/selected method.
- Evidence roles and exposure history must be preserved.
- Partition rules must be declared before validation begins.
- Good partitioning does not by itself eliminate lookahead or leakage.
